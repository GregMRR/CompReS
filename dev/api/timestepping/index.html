Time stepping

CompReS.Euler — Type
Euler()

The forward Euler scheme, order 1, one stage:

\[\begin{array}{c|c} 0 & \\ \hline & 1 \end{array}\]

It is unstable on undamped oscillating modes; use it for tests only (ADR-0005).

source
CompReS.RK2 — Type
RK2()

Heun's scheme, order 2, two stages:

\[\begin{array}{c|cc} 0 & & \\ 1 & 1 & \\ \hline & \tfrac12 & \tfrac12 \end{array}\]

It is unstable on undamped oscillating modes; use it for tests only (ADR-0005).

source
CompReS.RK4 — Type
RK4()

The classical Runge–Kutta scheme, order 4, four stages:

\[\begin{array}{c|cccc} 0 & & & & \\ \tfrac12 & \tfrac12 & & & \\ \tfrac12 & 0 & \tfrac12 & & \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & 1 & \\ \hline & \tfrac16 & \tfrac13 & \tfrac13 & \tfrac16 \end{array}\]

source
CompReS.RungeKuttaScheme — Type
RungeKuttaScheme

Supertype of the explicit Runge–Kutta schemes Euler, RK2, SSPRK3 and RK4. Each scheme is a Butcher table $(a_{ij}, b_i, c_i)$ with $s$ stages, read by butcher_table. For $\partial_t \hat{\mathbf{u}} = F(\hat{\mathbf{u}})$ one step reads

\[\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{(i)} = \hat{\mathbf{u}}_n + \Delta t \sum_{j<i} a_{ij} \mathbf{k}_j, \qquad \mathbf{k}_i = F(\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{(i)}), \qquad \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{n+1} = \hat{\mathbf{u}}_n + \Delta t \sum_i b_i \mathbf{k}_i .\]

source
CompReS.SSPRK3 — Type
SSPRK3()

The strong-stability-preserving scheme of Shu and Osher, order 3, three stages:

\[\begin{array}{c|ccc} 0 & & & \\ 1 & 1 & & \\ \tfrac12 & \tfrac14 & \tfrac14 & \\ \hline & \tfrac16 & \tfrac16 & \tfrac23 \end{array}\]

source
CompReS.TimeStepper — Type
TimeStepper(scheme::RungeKuttaScheme, integrating_factor::Bool, fields::NamedTuple; timer = nothing)

The time-stepping part of the Workspace: the scheme, the integrating-factor switch and the stage buffers for the spectral fields of a State, allocated once. step! reads it as workspace.stepper.

The fields are scheme, integrating_factor, nodes (the $c_i$ in the float type T of fields), tendencies (one set of spectral fields per stage, for $\mathbf{k}_i$), stage (a State whose fields hold the stage value $\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{(i)}$) and timer. The stage combinations of the Butcher table are precomputed as lists of additions and propagations.

timer is nothing or a TimerOutput of TimerOutputs.jl. With a TimerOutput, step! times each of its tasks into it, with a device synchronisation before and after each task (the profile mode of the runtime log, ADR-0010). The synchronisations slow a GPU run. With nothing, step! times nothing.

source
CompReS.butcher_table — Method
butcher_table(scheme::RungeKuttaScheme) -> (a, b, c)

The Butcher table of scheme with exact Rational{Int} entries: the strictly lower-triangular $s \times s$ matrix a, the weights b and the nodes c, with $c_1 = 0$.

CompReS.step! — Method
step!(state, problem, workspace, Δt) -> state

Advance state by one time step Δt with the explicit Runge–Kutta scheme and the integrating-factor switch of workspace.stepper (a TimeStepper), then add Δt to state.t and 1 to state.n.

The order of the calls in one step is:

  1. update_forcing!(problem.forcing, state, workspace, Δt), once: the forcing $\hat{\mathbf{f}}_n$ stays constant over the stages (ADR-0005).
  2. record_injection!(problem, state, workspace, Δt), with $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_n$ and $\hat{\mathbf{f}}_n$.
  3. The stages: rhs!(k, stage, problem, workspace) gives the nonlinear term and the forcing, $N(\hat{\mathbf{u}})$.

Without the integrating factor, each stage adds the linear terms with linear_tendency!, so $F = L\hat{\mathbf{u}} + N$ and the step is the scheme of RungeKuttaScheme. With the integrating factor (Lawson form), propagate! integrates the linear part exactly, $E(\tau) = e^{L\tau}$:

\[\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{(i)} = E(c_i\Delta t)\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_n + \Delta t \sum_{j<i} a_{ij}\, E\big((c_i - c_j)\Delta t\big)\, N(\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{(j)}), \qquad \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{n+1} = E(\Delta t)\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_n + \Delta t \sum_i b_i\, E\big((1 - c_i)\Delta t\big)\, N(\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{(i)}) .\]

Each scheme keeps its order with the integrating factor. In SSPRK3, $c_3 < c_2$, so the third stage applies the growth factor $E(-\Delta t/2)$ to $N(\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{(2)})$; it overflows when $\sigma\Delta t/2$ exceeds log(floatmax(T)) (88 for Float32) on a mode with rate $\sigma$.

When workspace.stepper.timer is a TimerOutput (profile mode, ADR-0010), the function times the tasks update_forcing!, record_injection!, rhs!, linear terms and stage combination into it.

problem provides the fields forcing and rates and a method of rhs!; rates takes linear_tendency! and propagate! methods. workspace provides the field stepper, and whatever rhs!, update_forcing! and record_injection! read. A test problem that defines these hooks runs through the same step! as the solver. The function allocates no array: it writes the stages into the buffers of workspace.stepper and the result into state.fields.
